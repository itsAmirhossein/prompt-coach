# Domain overlay — Writing, marketing & product communication

**Detect this domain:** copywriting, campaigns, content, PRDs, user stories, proposals, customer-facing replies.

## Optimization criteria
Audience precision, voice control by example, one message per artifact, concrete success behavior (what should the reader do/feel/decide?).

## Common prompt problems in this domain
- No audience, or a fake one (CON02): "write a blog post about our feature" — for buyers? developers? investors? Every downstream choice (vocabulary, length, angle) depends on this.
- Voice by adjective instead of by example (CLR02): "make it punchy and professional" is unfalsifiable. One pasted paragraph of the desired voice outperforms any adjective list — this is the domain where examples matter most.
- No reader-action (CLR03): what should the reader do after reading? Sign up, reply, approve, feel reassured? Without it the copy optimizes for sounding good.
- PRD/user-story asks without acceptance criteria (CLR03) or with solution smuggled into the problem statement (CON03).
- Customer-reply asks without the situation's constraints (CON02): what was promised, what policy applies, what tone the relationship warrants, what must NOT be committed to.
- Format underspecification for structured artifacts (FMT01): PRDs and proposals have expected sections — name them or reference a template.

## Recommended structure
```
Artifact: what's being written (email / landing section / PRD / reply).
Audience: who reads it, what they know, what they're skeptical of.
Goal: the one action or belief the piece should produce.
Voice: 1 short example paragraph of the desired tone (beats adjectives).
Musts / must-nots: claims to include, promises not to make, length.
[For PRDs: problem, evidence, non-goals, acceptance criteria as sections]
```
